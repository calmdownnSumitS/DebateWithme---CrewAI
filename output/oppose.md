The motion rests on a historical inaccuracy: the Mayan Long Count calendar—the primary wellspring of 2012 apocalyptic speculation—concluded on 12/21/12, not 12/12/12. There is no record of 12/12/12 ever being cited as a doomsday date in prophecies, religious teachings, or scientific forecasts; the day became an internet meme only after 12/21/12 had already achieved that status. The "reasons" cited—recursive numerology, New Age harmonics, and film promotions—are all retroactive justifications. Even the argument in favor concedes the numerology is "even if scientifically baseless" and satisfies a "craving for pattern-based meaning," which explicitly frames the belief as manufactured meaning rather than genuine prophetic assertion. To claim 12/12/12 was asserted as the Earth's last day is to conflate a post-hoc cultural joke with an actual prediction, and the supposed reasons collapse under their own anachronism and lack of historical grounding.
